About aerial / drone cams

Aerial and rooftop views trade detail for scope — whole districts, coastlines or valleys in one frame. They are the establishing shots of the webcam world, and the first place to look when weather is the thing you actually want to watch.

Much of this collection comes from dedicated operators — Drone Snap (4 feeds), Storyful News & Weather (4 feeds), and Polychronis Film (3 feeds) — channels that keep cameras running year-round rather than posting one-off clips.

Resolution figures here are measured, not scraped from titles: Camlisted records the real playback quality when a feed is opened. 10 of these feeds have been measured so far — 7 in 4K and 3 in full HD.

Condition tags mark what a clip actually shows, so you can jump straight to a scenario: currently 4 night scenes and 1 fire scene tagged here.

How many aerial / drone live cams are there?

8 live aerial / drone cameras are streaming right now, plus 24 recorded clips, with the deepest coverage in United States, Spain, France, and Maldives. The list is re-verified every night.

What are aerial / drone cams useful for?

Beyond watching for its own sake, fixed public cameras of this kind are widely used as reference and test footage — checking how detection or tracking models behave on real scenes instead of curated datasets.
