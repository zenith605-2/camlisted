About mountain cams

Mountain cams exist for two audiences: skiers checking snow before a trip, and everyone else watching clouds pour over a ridge at sunrise. Resorts run most of them, which means they tend to be well-maintained, high up, and pointed at the best view the operator could find. Time-lapse fans get more weather drama per hour here than any other category.

Much of this collection comes from dedicated operators — afarTV (14 feeds), Paesaggi Digitali (9 feeds), and フジヤマNAVI (8 feeds) — channels that keep cameras running year-round rather than posting one-off clips.

Resolution figures here are measured, not scraped from titles: Camlisted records the real playback quality when a feed is opened. 129 of these feeds have been measured so far — 25 in 4K, 12 in QHD, 74 in full HD, and 16 in HD.

The longest-running live camera in this set has been broadcasting continuously since 2019 — “Togwotee Mountain Lodge - SeeJH.com”.

Frequently asked questions

How many mountain live cams are there?

145 live mountain cameras are streaming right now, plus 5 recorded clips, with the deepest coverage in Japan, United States, Italy, and Indonesia. The list is re-verified every night.

What are mountain cams useful for?

Beyond watching for its own sake, fixed public cameras of this kind are widely used as reference and test footage — checking how detection or tracking models behave on real scenes instead of curated datasets.

Is it free to watch? Do I need an account?

Yes, and no account is needed. Every feed is a public YouTube stream embedded from its original channel — Camlisted links to sources and never hosts or downloads video.
