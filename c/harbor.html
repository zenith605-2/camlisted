About harbor cams

Harbor cams reward patience. Container ships slide in over twenty minutes, tugs fuss around them, ferries keep schedules you can set a clock by. Port authorities and waterfront hotels run most of these feeds, and ship-spotters pair them with AIS trackers to identify every vessel that passes.

Much of this collection comes from dedicated operators — 東京都伊豆・小笠原諸島港湾ライブカメラ (17 feeds), Vision-Environnement - Webcams LIVE (8 feeds), and Duluth Harbor Cam (6 feeds) — channels that keep cameras running year-round rather than posting one-off clips.

Resolution figures here are measured, not scraped from titles: Camlisted records the real playback quality when a feed is opened. 153 of these feeds have been measured so far — 27 in 4K, 14 in QHD, 69 in full HD, and 38 in HD.

Condition tags mark what a clip actually shows, so you can jump straight to a scenario: currently 1 night scene and 2 rain scenes tagged here.

The longest-running live camera in this set has been broadcasting continuously since 2019 — “Morro Bay Yacht Club - See.Cam”.

Frequently asked questions

How many harbor live cams are there?

170 live harbor cameras are streaming right now, plus 9 recorded clips, with the deepest coverage in United States, Japan, United Kingdom, and Spain. The list is re-verified every night.

What are harbor cams useful for?

Beyond watching for its own sake, fixed public cameras of this kind are widely used as reference and test footage — checking how detection or tracking models behave on real scenes instead of curated datasets.

Is it free to watch? Do I need an account?

Yes, and no account is needed. Every feed is a public YouTube stream embedded from its original channel — Camlisted links to sources and never hosts or downloads video.
