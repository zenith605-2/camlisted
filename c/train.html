About train / railway cams

Rail cams have one of the most devoted audiences on YouTube: level crossings, station platforms and trackside views where the payoff is a scheduled event you can wait for. Railfans keep timetables in the chat, and the long quiet stretches between trains are part of the appeal, not a bug.

Much of this collection comes from dedicated operators — Virtual Railfan (57 feeds), Steel Highway Railcams (26 feeds), and ふじみ野太郎 (19 feeds) — channels that keep cameras running year-round rather than posting one-off clips.

Resolution figures here are measured, not scraped from titles: Camlisted records the real playback quality when a feed is opened. 194 of these feeds have been measured so far — 13 in 4K, 9 in QHD, 146 in full HD, and 26 in HD.

Condition tags mark what a clip actually shows, so you can jump straight to a scenario: currently 12 night scenes and 1 snow scene tagged here.

The longest-running live camera in this set has been broadcasting continuously since 2020 — “いまのJR札幌駅／Live streaming from JR Sapporo Station 北海道ｏｎ天…”.

Frequently asked questions

How many train / railway live cams are there?

221 live train / railway cameras are streaming right now, plus 94 recorded clips, with the deepest coverage in Japan, United States, United Kingdom, and Brazil. The list is re-verified every night.

What are train / railway cams useful for?

Beyond watching for its own sake, fixed public cameras of this kind are widely used as reference and test footage — checking how detection or tracking models behave on real scenes instead of curated datasets.

Is it free to watch? Do I need an account?

Yes, and no account is needed. Every feed is a public YouTube stream embedded from its original channel — Camlisted links to sources and never hosts or downloads video.
