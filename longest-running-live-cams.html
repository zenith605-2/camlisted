What the numbers say

Most live cams do not last. Of the 2,393 broadcasting right now with a start time on record, the median has been up for just 233 days. Past that the field thins fast: 951 have run over a year, 440 over three, and only 130 have held a single unbroken broadcast for five years or more.

The current record holder is “Renesse aan Zee” from Hage Hosting in Netherlands, streaming without a break for 8 years and 84 days — since 2018. That is one continuous YouTube broadcast, not a channel that has been posting since then.

The five-year club skews geographically: United States (50), Japan (23), and Italy (13) account for much of it, and a handful of operators carry several each — Paesaggi Digitali (7), Umbria Webcam (6), and Range (5). Longevity tends to follow whoever treats the camera as infrastructure rather than a project.

Subject matter matters more than you might expect. Resort / Hotel cameras run a median 1 year and 86 days, while Other cameras manage 96 days. Cameras bolted to a building someone maintains outlast cameras set up for a season or a view that shifts.

Frequently asked questions

What counts as "continuously running"?

The clock is YouTube's own start time for the broadcast that is playing right now. If an operator stops and restarts a stream, YouTube issues a new video and the count begins again at zero — so every camera on this list has held a single unbroken broadcast for the age shown. A camera that has filmed the same view for a decade across several restarts will not appear near the top.

How often is this list updated?

Nightly. Camlisted re-checks every stream once a day, and a camera drops off this page as soon as its broadcast ends. That is the point of the list: everything here was still running at the last check.

Why are there so few very old streams?

Because staying live is hard. Across 2,393 live cameras with a known start time the median age is 233 days — power cuts, expired keys, storms and channel changes end most broadcasts long before their first birthday. The 130 cameras past five years are the exception, not the norm.
